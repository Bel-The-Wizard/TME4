## What the rules are

The DALAS course policy, in the teacher's words as Charbel received them (12 Oct 2026), allows AI tools "both during the TME sessions and for the project", with three conditions. First, "AI writes the code, but YOU are responsible for everything it does": in a data-mining pipeline the student must be aware of every decision that affects the results, such as "the fraction of the dataset used for training and testing", "the transformations applied to each variable", "how variables are interpreted", "the objective function", "the parameters of the chosen method" and "the number of repetitions of the experiment". Second, the teacher "strongly recommend[s] using these tools to generate or improve individual functions and small pieces of code step by step, rather than asking them to produce an entire analysis pipeline autonomously". Third, "demonstrating that you have a good knowledge of what your code is doing will be part of the evaluation."

What does apply is Sorbonne Université's practical guide on AI use (*Usage de l'IA à Sorbonne Université : le guide pratique*, August 2026). As retrieved tonight, it says that each teacher or teaching team sets its own policy and may encourage, regulate or forbid AI use (p. 8); that a student without explicit instructions should ask the teacher (p. 8); that in an assessment the teacher's or faculty's instructions apply (p. 4); that students should always state their use of AI systems, naming the system and the kind of use (p. 8); that using generative AI where it is forbidden constitutes fraud (p. 4); and that students remain responsible for every error in work submitted under their name and must never paste output without critical review and personal appropriation (pp. 8, 10). These quotations came through a web-fetch tool and should be checked against the PDF itself before being relied on.

## How AI was actually used

Claude was used throughout the project, and the share of the deliverable it produced is large. Charbel set the research question and its successive reformulations, chose the three-then-four problem families, rejected a mostly synthetic dataset and pushed the pivot to real data, made every cleaning decision (rules R1 to R6, including group-aware re-splitting over deduplication and flagging over removal), removed Q5 and terminated Q6, and imposed working rules on the AI: no code without a per-function specification, every claim tied to a source, nothing written to project files without approval. He reviewed and questioned the work at each step, and edited six harvest files himself on 5 Oct.

Against that, Claude wrote most of the code and most of the text. The harvest pipeline, the problem generators, the audit script and the final-dataset builder were written by Claude, with Charbel reviewing and approving. The analysis was first written as a commented skeleton on 10 Oct; on 11 Oct at 16:19 Charbel asked Claude to implement all of it, and Claude wrote and ran the full analysis between 16:20 and 22:30, found and fixed the bugs, and wrote `ANALYSIS_RESULTS.md`. Most design documents (`PROJECT_DESIGN.md`, `HARVEST.md`, `QnA_what_to_do.md`, `CLEANUP_LOG.md`) were drafted by Claude. Tonight, Charbel wrote the summary himself (`1_SUMMARY_LARGE.tex`), drawing on Claude's chat explanations, several passages almost word for word. Claude reformatted it, wrote parts of the summaries, including the Q4, Conclusions and Next Steps sections, and built the whole PowerPoint presentation.

## Is this a violation?

Under this policy, using AI is not itself a violation, however large its share; the policy allows it explicitly. What the policy makes Charbel answerable for is every decision in the pipeline, and on that point the record is uneven. Many decisions are his: the 70/30 stratified split by unit, the group-aware re-split (R2), the median aggregation of features over inputs, balanced accuracy with leave-one-group-out validation, and the statistical tests of Q2 to Q4 (`docs/DECISIONS.md` §11). Others were proposed by Claude and confirmed by Charbel on 11 Oct: COVARIANCE site weights of 1 in the factor graph, the classifier's regularisation C = 1, the 0.8 threshold for grouping correlated features, no log transforms, at least four sizes per Q4 line, the per-side spin features, and no central charge for four spin models. These are exactly the kinds of choice the policy names, and all of them now carry his decision. They remain open until he has checked the results they produce (see the update of 21 Oct below). On method, the analysis functions were specified one by one in a skeleton (10 Oct), but Claude then implemented the whole analysis in a single six-hour run (11 Oct, 16:20 to 22:30), which is the pattern the teacher advises against, though does not forbid. Charbel walked through `analysis_common.py` with Claude; the other analysis files have not yet been walked through. The AI use is declared by this file, which is part of the submission.

## What would make it clean

Two things, both from the course policy. First, check the results produced under the choices listed above, then settle each choice with its reason and re-run the analysis if any changes (`next_steps.md`, item 2). Second, go through each analysis file until every function and every parameter can be explained without help, starting with `mixed_models.py` and `q3_classify.py`, which carry the least familiar methods; this is what the policy says will be evaluated.

## Update, Sat 17 Oct 2026

The two-page Deliverable 1 report (`DELIVERABLE_1/REPORT_TME4.pdf`) was revised with Claude in one session. Charbel directed it and decided each change: the group and TME line, describing the variables per input and grouping them under the four structures, excluding the periodic XY spin chains rather than re-harvesting them, deferring how a problem's inputs are summarised to the analysis, and framing the Hodge step as a first instance of his Logical State Space. Claude checked the report against the course brief and lectures, traced every choice back to the project files, and drafted the text, which Charbel read and corrected before approving. Claude also updated three slides (6, 7 and 12) to match the report.

The session corrected the record on three decisions. The 70/30 proportion of the split has no recorded reason; Charbel now states it as a convention (the split by unit, which prevents leakage, remains his). The class-balance rule, which randomly reduced the majority class to the size of the minority class in 51 problems, was a default proposed in the harvest design documents drafted by Claude and listed as open on 5 Oct, but never settled; the pipeline ran with it. Charbel reviewed it on 17 Oct and decided to keep it as applied, and the report now states it. The median aggregation of features over inputs, listed above as his decision, is now under review: he doubts that one median can represent a problem whose values may form several groups, and the report leaves that choice to the analysis.
